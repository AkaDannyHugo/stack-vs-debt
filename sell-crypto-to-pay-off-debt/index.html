Should I sell my crypto to pay off debt, or keep holding?

Short answer: If your debt charges 20-30% a year, your crypto has to grow faster than that, every year, after taxes, just to break even. In the numbers, clearing the highest-rate debt removes a known cost, while holding keeps an uncertain outcome. The useful question is how big that uncertainty is, and for how long.

The one comparison that matters

Every month you keep a credit card balance, you pay a known cost: the interest. Every month you keep your crypto, you get an uncertain result: it might go up, flat, or down a lot. So the decision is a known cost vs an uncertain gain.

A card at 24% APR compounds to about 26.8% a year. At 29.99% APR it is about 34.5% a year. That is the hurdle your coins must clear just to tie.

Example (made-up numbers)

Someone holds $20,000 in crypto and owes $12,000 across two cards at 24% and 27% APR. Interest is roughly $250 a month. If they hold for a year and crypto stays flat, they have paid about $3,000 in interest for nothing. If crypto doubles, holding wins. If it halves, they now owe more and hold less.

The question isn't really sell vs. hold. It's "what happens to me in each of those three cases, and can I live with the worst one?"

Things that change the answer

  • Monthly shortfall. If you already sell some crypto every month to cover bills, you are selling anyway, often at bad times. Clearing debt first can shrink that shortfall.
  • Taxes. Selling at a gain can create capital gains tax. Long-term holdings (over a year in the US) are usually taxed less. Rules vary, so check with a tax professional.
  • Debt type. A 6% car loan and a 28% credit card are very different. On interest alone, the math puts the highest APR first.
  • Promotional 0% balances. A 0% balance transfer changes the math until the promo ends.
  • Sleep. If a 50% drop would force you into worse debt, the risk is bigger than the chart shows.

Three strategies the calculator compares

  1. Pay now: sell enough to pay off the highest-APR balances and hold the rest.
  2. Wait: hold everything and pay minimums; this comes out ahead only if price gains beat the interest.
  3. Sell monthly: pay extra each month from small sales.

Stack vs Debt runs all three against a crash, flat, and growth path so you can see the range, not just the hopeful case.

Common questions

Is it smart to sell crypto to pay off credit card debt?

The math leans that way when the card APR is high: a 24% APR card costs about 27% a year, so crypto has to beat that after tax just to break even. Run your own numbers.

Do I pay tax if I sell crypto to pay off debt?

In the US, selling crypto at a gain is usually a taxable event regardless of what you spend the money on. Losses can offset gains. Check with a tax professional.

Should I sell all my crypto or just part?

Selling only enough to clear the highest-rate debt and holding the rest is one case to model. A calculator that compares strategies under different price paths shows the trade-off.
