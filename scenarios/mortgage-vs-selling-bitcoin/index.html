The growth needed to keep pace

  • In the math, keeping the holdings comes out ahead of paying the 6.5% debt only if they grow faster than about 6.7% a year, after tax and fees.
  • Today the holdings ($150,000) cover about 99% of the debt ($150,000) after a 1% fee.
  • To clear all of the debt with what is held, the crypto would need to rise about 1% (the calculator's break-even, shown as a percentage, not a price).

Taxes

Selling bitcoin at a gain is usually a taxable event in the US no matter what the money pays for; the examples here set aside 0% for tax, so taxes are not in these numbers. Mortgage interest may be deductible for people who itemize, which lowers its after-tax cost. In the US, assets held a year or less are usually taxed as ordinary income and longer holdings at lower long-term rates. Selling lots with losses can offset gains. Tax rules vary; check with a tax professional.

Assumptions

  • Example holdings: $150,000 BTC. Values in dollars; no coin or share prices are used.
  • Minimum payments: Mortgage $948/mo (cards: interest + 1% of the balance; loans: the regular payment over a typical remaining term). In every strategy they are paid by selling holdings, as in the calculator's "monthly gap", so all strategies have the same cash flow.
  • "Sell a bit extra monthly" sells $12,500 a month toward the highest APR. Trading fee 1% per sale; tax set aside 0%.
  • Price paths are the calculator's three scenarios, not forecasts. The model runs 36 months.
