Should I sell bitcoin to pay off my credit card?

Short answer: Holding bitcoin instead of paying off a card only wins if BTC rises faster than your card's interest compounds. On a 25% APR card that's roughly 28% a year, before taxes. The fastest way to decide is to find your break-even price: the BTC price you'd need by a given date for waiting to have been worth it.

Find your break-even BTC price

Break-even is the price at which holding and selling end up the same. Below it, selling earlier would have been better. Above it, holding paid off.

Rough method: take the interest you'd pay while waiting, plus any extra coins you'd sell to cover minimums, and ask how much BTC would need to rise to cover that. Stack vs Debt does this for you and shows it as a price and a multiple (for example "BTC needs to be 1.3x today's price in 12 months").

Example (made-up numbers)

0.3 BTC held, $9,000 on one card at 25% APR, $300/month minimum. Over 12 months, interest is roughly $2,100. If BTC goes nowhere, waiting cost about $2,100. If BTC drops 50%, the stack covers far less of the debt and the balance is still growing.

Questions to ask yourself first

  • Are you selling sats every month to cover bills already? Then you're a forced seller, and debt makes that worse.
  • Would a 50% drawdown push you into missed payments or a cash advance?
  • Is any of your BTC held over a year (lower long-term capital gains rate in the US)?
  • Could a 0% balance transfer buy time without selling?

A middle path

Many holders sell just enough to clear the highest-APR card, keep the rest, and stop the interest bleeding. You keep exposure without paying 25%+ for the privilege.

Common questions

What is a bitcoin break-even price for debt?

It's the BTC price you'd need by a certain date for holding (and paying interest) to match selling now to pay off the debt. Below that price, selling would have been better.

Is it better to borrow against bitcoin to pay off credit cards?

A BTC-backed loan can have a lower rate but adds liquidation risk if the price falls. Compare it against simply selling part of your stack.

How much interest does a credit card cost per year?

A 25% APR compounded monthly is about 28% a year. On a $9,000 balance that's roughly $2,000-2,500 a year if you only pay minimums.
