Should I sell stocks to pay off credit card debt?

Short answer: Stocks have historically returned around 7-10% a year on average (historical, not a prediction), while credit cards often charge 20-30%. Over most realistic periods the card wins that race, so selling taxable investments to clear high-APR cards is usually the better math. Retirement accounts are different: early withdrawals carry taxes and penalties.

Expected return vs known cost

Paying off a 22% card removes a known 22% cost; market returns are not known in advance. Keeping stocks while carrying the card comes out ahead only if the market beats 22% after tax, a high bar next to the long-run historical average (historical, not a prediction).

Example (made-up numbers)

$15,000 in a taxable index fund, $8,000 on a 22% card. Selling $8,000 (plus enough to cover any tax on gains) clears the card and saves around $1,800 a year in interest. Keeping it all invested only wins if the fund gains more than that on the whole $15,000 position after tax.

Things that change the math

  • Taxable vs retirement accounts. Selling in a taxable brokerage account may create capital gains. Withdrawing from a 401(k) or IRA early usually triggers income tax plus a 10% additional tax (US) and permanently loses tax-advantaged room. Tax rules vary, so check with a tax professional.
  • Losses can offset gains (check with a tax professional).
  • Cash buffer. Without one, new charges can refill the card.
  • Ongoing charges. Paying off a card that keeps getting charged resets the clock.

Common questions

Is it better to invest or pay off credit card debt?

With card APRs of 20%+, paying off the card usually comes out ahead of investing in the math, because the interest cost is known and market returns are not. An employer 401(k) match is a common exception in the math, since the match is an immediate return on contributions.

Should I cash out my 401(k) to pay off credit cards?

Early withdrawals can cost income tax plus a 10% additional tax and lost growth, so the math usually favors other sources first. Plan rules and exceptions vary; check with a tax professional.

Do I pay taxes when I sell stocks to pay debt?

Selling in a taxable account at a gain usually creates capital gains tax; held over a year is typically taxed at a lower rate in the US. Tax rules vary; check with a tax professional.
