$200,000 mortgage at 3.5% or 6.5%: sell stocks or keep the loan? A 1-3 year view

Worked example with made-up numbers, computed with the same engine as the calculator. Not anyone's real finances.

This is a 1-3 year snapshot, not a full mortgage payoff. The calculator models 36 months, so a 30-year loan is still mostly unpaid at the end in every strategy except paying it off now; the minimum payment is the regular monthly principal-and-interest payment.

Two example rates are shown, 3.5% and 6.5%, because the comparison changes between them.

Taxes

Selling in a taxable brokerage account at a gain usually creates capital gains tax; retirement accounts follow different rules. Mortgage interest may be deductible for people who itemize, which lowers its after-tax cost. In the US, assets held a year or less are usually taxed as ordinary income and longer holdings at lower long-term rates. Selling lots with losses can offset gains. Tax rules vary; check with a tax professional.

Assumptions

  • Example holdings: $200,000 Brokerage (stocks). Values in dollars; no coin or share prices are used.
  • Minimum payments: Mortgage at 3.5% $898/mo; at 6.5% $1,264/mo (cards: interest + 1% of the balance; loans: the regular payment over a typical remaining term). In every strategy they are paid by selling holdings, as in the calculator's "monthly gap", so all strategies have the same cash flow.
  • "Sell a bit extra monthly" sells $16,650 a month toward the highest APR. Trading fee 1% per sale; tax set aside 0%.
  • Price paths are the calculator's three scenarios, not forecasts. The model runs 36 months.
