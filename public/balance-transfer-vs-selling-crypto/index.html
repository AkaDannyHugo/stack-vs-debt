Should I do a 0% balance transfer or sell my crypto to pay off my card?

Short answer: A balance transfer swaps a 20-30% APR for a one-time fee (often 3-5%) and a deadline. In the numbers, it comes out ahead of selling mainly when the balance can be cleared from income before the promo ends. When it can't, the leftover balance goes back to a high APR and the crypto is still exposed to price swings.

What a balance transfer really costs

  • The fee. Usually a percentage of the amount moved, often 3-5%, added to the new balance on day one.
  • The promo length. Commonly somewhere around 12-21 months at 0%. The clock starts when the account opens or the transfer posts, not when you feel ready.
  • The APR after it ends. Whatever is left at the end switches to the card's regular rate, which is often 20%+.
  • Approval. You need to be approved, the limit may be lower than your balance, and most issuers won't let you transfer between two of their own cards.

Where you still hold it, that $8,000 of crypto is worth about $4,000 after a 50% crash, $8,000 if prices stay flat, or $16,000 if they double. Selling now gives up both the loss and the upside.

Promo terms vary. Tax rules vary; check with a tax professional. Assumes no new spending on the card. In the "sell now" row, the monthly payment money stays in your pocket instead.

The "can I pay it off in time?" test

Divide the transferred balance (including the fee) by the number of promo months. In the example, $8,320 ÷ 15 = about $555 a month. Now compare that with the surplus you really have each month after bills, not the one you hope to have. If the real number is lower, the transfer only delays the problem, and part of the balance lands back at a high rate.

A hybrid some people model: transfer first, with a checkpoint

Another case worth running is a transfer paid from income, with a checkpoint set in advance. For example (made-up numbers): "by month 6 the balance is under about $5,000; if it isn't, sell enough crypto to close the gap." In the math, the 0% window does the work while payments stay on schedule, and the checkpoint shows how much would need to be sold if they don't.

Common questions

Is a balance transfer better than selling crypto?

In the math, it tends to come out ahead when the balance can be paid off from income before the 0% period ends. The fee is usually much smaller than a year of card interest. If it isn't cleared in time, the rest goes back to a high APR while the crypto is still exposed to price swings. Which one fits depends on details only you know.

What happens if I don't pay it off before the promo ends?

The remaining balance starts charging the card's regular APR, often 20% or more. You don't lose the progress you made, but the leftover balance gets expensive again.

Does a balance transfer hurt my credit score?

Applying for a new card usually means a hard inquiry and a newer average account age, which can lower a score a little for a while. Paying the balance down can help by lowering how much of your credit you use.
