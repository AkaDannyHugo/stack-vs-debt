Should I sell crypto to pay off my car loan or student loans?

Short answer: Low-rate debt changes the math. At 4-7% APR, holdings only need to beat about 4-7% a year after tax for holding to come out ahead, so the numbers turn mostly on cash flow, taxes and price risk, not interest. What selling mainly buys here is a freed-up monthly payment.

A $14,000 truck loan at 4.5% with a $350 monthly payment (about 44 payments left). The owner holds $15,000 of BTC that is up 20% from what they paid. Over the rest of the loan, total interest is only about $1,200.

In this example the interest saved is about $1,200 at most, while the swing in the crypto is many times that. So the numbers come down to how much the $350 a month matters to the budget, and how large a drop the plan can absorb.

Things that change the answer

  • Cash-flow relief. If the payment is squeezing your budget or pushing you onto cards, freeing it up can matter more than the interest.
  • Tax on gains. In the US, selling crypto held a year or less is taxed as ordinary income; held longer, gains usually get lower long-term rates. Selling at a loss can offset other gains. Rules vary, so check with a tax professional.
  • Federal student loans. Prepaying has no penalty, but money you put in can't be taken back if you later need income-driven repayment, deferment or forbearance. If you're working toward forgiveness, paying early may not help. Refinancing federal loans into a private loan gives those protections up for good. Check studentaid.gov for current rules.
  • Car loan terms. Some loans have prepayment fees; most don't. Check your contract.
  • Emergency fund. A paid-off car doesn't help if a surprise bill goes on a 28% card next month.

Which debt the math puts first

When a credit card balance is also in the picture, each dollar sent to a 25% card saves far more interest than the same dollar sent to a 4.5% car loan. On interest alone, the highest APR comes first in the math.

Common questions

Should I sell bitcoin to pay off a 4% car loan?

The interest saved is usually small, so the numbers turn on cash flow, taxes and price risk. Selling frees the monthly payment; holding only has to beat about 4% a year after tax. A calculator can show both sides with your own numbers.

Is it smart to use crypto gains to pay off student loans?

It depends on the rate, the tax on the gains, and whether the loans are federal. Tax rules vary; check with a tax professional. Federal loans come with repayment options and possible forgiveness that early payoff can make less useful. Check studentaid.gov before prepaying.

Should I pay off my car or credit card first with crypto?

On interest alone, the credit card, because its APR is usually several times higher: each dollar paid on the highest-rate debt saves the most interest. Cash flow and loan terms can change the picture.
