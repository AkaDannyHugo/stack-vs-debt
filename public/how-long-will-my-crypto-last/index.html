How long will my crypto last if I keep selling to cover my bills?

Short answer: Your runway is roughly your stack's value divided by what you sell each month, but debt interest and price swings change it fast. In a falling market you sell more coins for the same dollars, so runway shrinks faster than the simple math suggests. Model at least a crash, a flat, and a growth path.

The simple version

Runway (months) ≈ stack value ÷ monthly amount you sell. $18,000 stack, selling $750 a month: about 24 months, if price stays flat.

Why the simple version is too optimistic

  • Price drops mean each $750 costs more coins. A 50% fall over a year can cut runway by a third or more.
  • Interest grows the gap. If minimums rise with your balance, you sell more each month.
  • Fees and taxes take a slice of every sale (tax rules vary; check with a tax professional).

What extends runway in the model

  1. Clearing the highest-APR debt first lowers minimums and interest.
  2. A smaller monthly gap: $100/month less selling adds months.
  3. A "floor" price decided in advance changes the plan at a known point rather than in a panic.

Common questions

How do I calculate my crypto runway?

Divide your stack's value by your monthly sales, then adjust for price falls, interest, and fees. A calculator that runs several price paths gives a more honest range.

Is it bad to sell crypto every month to pay bills?

It isn't automatically bad, but it makes you a forced seller in down markets. Reducing high-interest debt first usually shrinks how much you need to sell.

What happens if my crypto runs out before my debt is paid?

The remaining shortfall typically lands back on your cards. Seeing that month in advance lets you change course earlier.
